## 答题逻辑（中文，3–5 步）
1. 原则：AI 做规模化的活（洞察、初筛、提效）；涉及人的判断留给人。
2. 受监管决策必须可解释、可审计——黑箱即使对也不能用。
3. AI 只能是 assistant / drafter；决策逻辑要确定、可审计。
4. AI 参与的每一步都要可逆、可验证。
5. 证据：你的 AI 服务的治理规则（PA-07.2）；你负责贷款服务和催收的技术（PA-04.1）——但**没有在 UOB 把 AI 用于信贷决策**。

## 30s
> AI should do the scalable work — insight, first-pass screening, drafting — and leave judgment calls that affect people to people. In lending and risk, a decision has to be explainable and auditable; a black box, even a correct one, can't be used. So AI assists, the decision logic stays deterministic, and every AI step is reversible and verifiable. That's the same rule I set for my AI code-review service: AI advises, humans decide.

## ATL — 60s
> Architecturally, I'd keep AI out of the decision path and put it beside it. The decision engine stays rule-based and versioned, so every outcome can be reproduced and explained. AI produces inputs — a risk flag, a summary, a suggested next action — that are logged with the model version and the prompt, and a human or a deterministic rule decides whether to use them. Every AI step must be reversible and verifiable. That's the pattern in my AI code-review service: the agent comments, SonarQube and humans gate, and only a human can merge.

## SM — 60s
> In lending, trust is the product, so I'd be careful where AI sits. AI is good at the scalable work: spotting patterns, first-pass screening, drafting, finding the cases a human should look at. But decisions that affect a customer — approval, pricing, collections treatment — have to be explainable to the customer and the regulator, so the decision logic stays deterministic and auditable, with a human accountable. That's the line I set in my own AI service: it advises, it never decides, and it went through formal architecture and risk approval before production. I own the technology for retail lending servicing and collections at UOB, so I know how sensitive those decisions are.
