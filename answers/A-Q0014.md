## 答题逻辑（中文，3–5 步）
1. 场景：两条平台线的发布窗口撞车。
2. 原则：不先升级。
3. 做法：拉齐需求、项目、技术负责人，谈 MVP，确认真实依赖，定出各方都接受的顺序。
4. 结果：决定由受影响的人自己做出，而不是靠上级压下去。

## 30s
> With two platform lines, release schedules sometimes collide. When that happens I don't escalate first. I bring the requirement owners, project leads and tech leads together, agree the MVP scope for each change, confirm the real dependencies, and set a sequence both sides accept. The priority call is owned by the people affected, not imposed through escalation.

## ATL — 60s
> My role in these conflicts is often to bring technical facts. When two lines want the same release window, the first question is whether the dependency is real. I go through the changes with the tech leads: what actually has to ship together, and what can be split or released separately. Once the real dependencies are clear, the MVP for each side and the sequence usually follow, and the requirement owners can agree on facts rather than positions. If the same two lines keep colliding, I also ask whether that coupling is something the architecture should remove.

## TPM — 60s

> **S** — On one of our payment projects, offshore developers in India and business users in Singapore kept clashing. Requirements changed often, and expectations weren't clear on either side.
> **T** — As technical delivery manager, my job was to get delivery stable again — not to referee each argument.

Before each sprint, I ran a requirement-clarification workshop with the BA, the dev lead, QA and the business users, so everyone agreed the acceptance criteria and scope up front. For late changes, I added a change-impact assessment, so the business could see the delivery impact before deciding.
> **R** — Escalations dropped, trust improved on both sides, and sprint delivery became much more stable.

## SM — 60s
> I treat these conflicts as a portfolio question. Two platform lines — cross-border payments and retail lending — compete for the same people and release windows. I set the principle: the people affected make the call, based on MVP scope and the real dependencies, and I hold everyone to the sequence they agree. Escalation is the exception, not the method. That keeps decisions fast, keeps the leads accountable, and means my own time goes to the conflicts that genuinely need a trade-off at my level.
