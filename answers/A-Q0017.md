## 答题逻辑（中文，3–5 步）
1. 过渡句："The closest example I have is a production incident."
2. 顺序：先止损 → 再查根因 → 最后永久修复。
3. 说清你本人做了什么（X-06：升级、沟通、RCA、事后报告）。
4. 收尾讲教训。
5. **具体案例（SB-04）要等收进 03 才能放进正文**；目前正文只讲流程。

## 30s
> The closest example I have is a production incident rather than a failed release. My teams own production support, so I run P1 to P3 incidents end to end: escalate, communicate with stakeholders, contain the business impact first, then find the real root cause, and finally drive a permanent fix and the post-incident report. The lesson I apply every time: contain, then root-cause, then prevent recurrence.

## ATL — 60s
> （SB-04 入库前）I start from the evidence. With our Splunk-based transaction monitoring and the logs, I trace the transaction across our hub and the downstream systems to see exactly where it stopped — in our system, in the downstream system, or in the channel between them. Then I separate containment from the fix: recover the affected transactions first, so the business impact stops, and only then work on the root cause and a permanent change, so the same failure can't recur. The order matters: under pressure, teams often jump to the fix and leave customers waiting.

## TPM — 60s
> （SB-04 入库前）In a P1 my job is coordination and discipline. My teams own production support — there's no separate SRE team — so I run the incident end to end within the bank's incident framework. I escalate, keep the business and stakeholders updated, and drive the investigation, the workaround and the permanent fix while tracking the SLA. Containment comes first: stop the business impact and recover the affected transactions. After recovery I lead the RCA and the post-incident report, and I provide the technical incident analysis that feeds the bank's regulatory reporting. The lesson I apply every time: contain, then find the real root cause, then prevent recurrence.
>
>【SD-02 入库后启用——你确认属实的指挥做法】In the first fifteen minutes I'm the single point of coordination: I open a war-room channel, get the on-call engineer on, and decide within ten minutes whether we can hold service or need to fail over. I assign one engineer to logs, one to database and messaging health, one to downstream dependencies, with an hourly sync. The first stakeholder update goes out within thirty minutes, then every hour. If it isn't resolved in the first hour, I bring in DevOps or security early. High-severity issues have to be resolved within half a day, and a blameless postmortem follows within 48 hours.

## SM — 60s
> What matters to me is that every incident makes the platform stronger. With no separate SRE team, my teams own production, so accountability is clear. Every P1 ends with an RCA and a permanent fix, and the lessons feed back into our Splunk monitoring and our SLIs and SLOs. I also make sure the technical incident analysis is solid enough to support the bank's regulatory reporting — in a MAS-regulated bank, how you handle an incident matters as much as how fast you fix it.
