## 答题逻辑（中文，3–5 步）
1. 直接回答：两者都是，而且是刻意的。
2. 正式角色：VP / TDM，通过 squad lead 和 EM 带 25 人。
3. 不是纯管理：负责架构、每日 review、重大变更时写核心模块；AI 服务是你自己建的。
4. 按目标岗位调整重心。

## 30s
> Both, deliberately. Formally I'm a VP and Technical Delivery Manager, leading 25 engineers through squad leads and engineering managers. But I'm not a pure manager: I own the architecture, review code every day, and write the core modules when significant changes land — most recently, I built our AI code-review service myself. So I'd describe myself as a hands-on technical lead with delivery and people responsibility.

## ATL — 60s
> Mostly an architect who still codes. My title is Technical Delivery Manager, but day to day I own the architecture of our payment-initiation hub, co-design solutions with the engineers, and review code daily, with the core modules in depth. When significant changes land, I write the core myself — on the hub that was transaction initiation, the permission matrix and status processing. And this year I designed and built our AI code-review service end to end. The delivery and people side runs largely through my squad leads, which is what frees my time for the technical work.

## TPM — 60s
> Primarily a delivery lead with real technical depth. I own end-to-end delivery for my platforms — planning, RAID, SIT and UAT sign-off, and taking every production release through CAB — and I lead 25 engineers through squad leads and engineering managers. What makes me different from a pure project manager is that I still own the architecture and review code daily, so I can read the risk in a change myself rather than relying on status reports.

## SM — 60s
> A leader of leaders who stays close to the technology. I lead 25 engineers across two platform lines through squad leads and engineering managers, and I hold decision or joint-decision authority on technology investment, vendors and SOWs. I'm deliberately not a pure manager: I own the architecture and still review code daily. That closeness is what lets me make good technical calls quickly.
