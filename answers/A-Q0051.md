## 答题逻辑（中文，3–5 步）
1. 先看懂再动：分成三组——留、清理、替换。
2. 只修挡路的：拖慢业务、经常出事、卡住流程的先修。
3. 前几个月不谈大重写：修真痛点、稳住系统、攒下信任。
4. 被追问"你做过吗"：Baidu 接手（PA-17.1）；ISO 20022 分阶段共存（PA-01.1）。

## 30s
> First, I don't touch anything until I understand it. I sort the system into three groups: what works and stays, what works but needs cleaning up, and what has to be replaced. Second, I fix only what is in the way — what slows the business, breaks often or blocks a process. Third, no talk of big rewrites in the first few months: fix the real pain, stabilise the system, earn trust. Then we talk about where it should go.

## ATL — 60s
> Technically, I start with evidence: architecture and dependency maps, the incident history, monitoring data and the code itself, reviewing the core modules first. That tells me where the real risk is. Then I sort components into keep, clean up and replace, and fix what's in the way first — the parts causing incidents or blocking delivery. Big rewrites wait until the system is stable and I understand why it was built that way. When change is needed, I do it in reversible steps, like the phased coexistence we used to move our payment hub to ISO 20022 with no service interruption.

## TPM — 60s
> I'd treat the first months as a stabilisation plan agreed with the business, not a technical project. First, understand the system, its incidents and its stakeholders. Then a short list of fixes, ranked by business impact — what slows the business, breaks often or blocks a process — delivered through the normal release and change gates. I report progress visibly, so trust builds. Only after that do we agree a longer roadmap, and any modernisation runs in reversible phases, as we did for ISO 20022.

## SM — 60s
> First, I don't touch anything until I understand it. I go through the system and sort it into three groups: what works and stays, what works but needs cleaning up, and what has to be replaced. Second, I only fix what is in the way: anything slowing the business down, breaking often or blocking a process goes first; everything else waits. Third, I don't talk about big rewrites in the first few months. I fix the real pain, make the system stable and earn the trust — after that, we can talk about where it should go. I've taken over systems before: at Baidu I took over a merchant-side order system as project leader, re-architected it on Dubbo microservices, and delivered two months ahead of plan.
