## 答题逻辑（中文，3–5 步）
1. 直接回答 Yes：每次生产发布前，两条平台线的 CRQ 都由你本人准备，并在 CAB 讲解（PA-03.5）。
2. 讲解内容：go-live plan、risk controls、rollback plan、impact assessment、SIT/UAT sign-off、database changes。
3. 为什么重要：MAS 监管下的可审计性。
4. 按岗位收尾：ATL 讲技术论证，TPM 讲流程，SM 讲治理怎样反过来提速。

## 30s
> Yes. Before every production release on both my platform lines, I prepare the change request myself and present it at the Change Advisory Board: the go-live plan, risk controls, rollback plan, impact assessment, SIT and UAT sign-off, and any database changes. In a MAS-regulated bank, auditability is non-negotiable, so the evidence trail is part of the release.

## ATL — 60s
> Yes — and I make sure the technical case is sound before it reaches CAB. I prepare the change request myself for every production release on both platform lines. From the technical side, it has to prove the change is safe: the scope, the database changes, the test evidence — SIT and UAT sign-off, plus automated regression and security testing from the pipeline — the implementation steps, and a rollback plan with a clear trigger. Because I review the code and own the architecture, I can answer CAB's technical questions directly rather than relaying them, which saves a lot of back-and-forth.

## TPM — 60s
> Yes. Every production release on my two platform lines goes through change approval, and I own it personally. I prepare the change request myself — in our system it's a CRQ — and I present it at the Change Advisory Board. It covers the go-live plan, risk controls, rollback plan, impact assessment, test results with SIT and UAT sign-off, and any database changes. Before that, I require SIT and UAT sign-off on every release, and we run secure SDLC in line with MAS TRM, so the evidence already exists when we get to CAB. In a regulated bank auditability is non-negotiable — the evidence trail is part of the release, not an afterthought.

## SM — 60s
> Yes — and I see change governance as an enabler, not a brake. I present every production release of my two platform lines at CAB myself, so I know where approvals get stuck and why. When the evidence is complete — go-live plan, risk controls, rollback, impact assessment, SIT and UAT sign-off — the CAB discussion is about risk, not paperwork. That discipline is what lets us keep a steady release cadence, every two to three months, in a MAS-regulated bank. In a new organisation, I'd look first at how much of that evidence the pipeline can produce automatically.
