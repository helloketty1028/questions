## 答题逻辑（中文，3–5 步）
1. 第一：凡是会伤到业务的先做——生产事故、合规期限。
2. 第二：业务已经承诺的事——上线日、对客户的承诺。
3. 第三：其余进 backlog，定期和业务一起排。
4. 证据：两条平台线排期冲突时的 MVP 排序做法（X-02）。

## 30s
> Anything that breaks the business comes first — production issues and compliance deadlines; those don't wait. Then what the business has already committed to — a launch date, a client promise. Everything else goes into the backlog, and I review it with the business regularly.

## TPM — 60s
> Three tiers. Anything that breaks the business comes first — production issues and compliance deadlines. Those don't wait, and my teams own production support, so P1 to P3 incidents always jump the queue. Then what the business has already committed to: a launch date, a client promise, a regulatory release. Everything else goes into the backlog, which I review with the business regularly. When two committed items still collide — as happens between my two platform lines, cross-border payments and retail lending — I agree MVP scope with the requirement, project and technical leads, confirm the real dependencies, and set a sequence everyone accepts.

## SM — 60s
> I make the rule explicit so it isn't decided by whoever shouts loudest. Production stability and compliance come first, always. Committed business dates come second. Everything else is a backlog that the business owns with me, reviewed on a fixed rhythm. And I protect some capacity for BAU on purpose: in my experience, when every person is booked on projects, the incidents eat the plan anyway. At UOB my teams run both — production support and regulated projects — across two platform lines, so this trade-off is weekly, not occasional.
