## 答题逻辑（中文，3–5 步）
1. 不用两阶段提交。
2. Outbox：业务变更和事件写在同一个本地事务里。
3. 幂等消费：唯一 ID + 唯一约束。
4. Saga：多步流程，每步都有补偿动作。
5. 对账作为最后一道控制；【SA-01 入库后】讲你 hub 的做法。

## 30s
> I avoid two-phase commit and combine patterns. Outbox: write the business change and the event in one local transaction, and publish separately. Idempotent consumers: every message has a unique ID with a unique constraint, so a retry is a no-op. Sagas with compensating actions for multi-step flows. And in banking, reconciliation is the final control.

## ATL — 60s
> I avoid two-phase commit — it's fragile and doesn't scale across services. Instead, four layers. One, the outbox pattern: the service writes the business change and the outgoing event in the same local transaction, and a separate relay publishes the event, so you never update the database and lose the message. Two, idempotent consumers: every message carries a unique ID, and the consumer records processed IDs under a unique constraint, so a redelivery is a no-op. Three, for multi-step flows, a saga, with a compensating action for each step. Four, reconciliation as the final control: we match against the ledger or the counterparty and route breaks to an exception queue. I built reconciliation into a payment subsystem at VV for exactly that reason.
