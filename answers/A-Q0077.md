1. 四步：找到压力来源 → 定可行方案 → 调动所有支持 → 严格按计划执行。
2. 证据：P1 事故（X-06）、SWIFT 截止日（PA-01.1）。
3. 收尾：压力下我是让团队稳住的那个人。

## 30s

In payments that's normal — P1 incidents and fixed regulatory deadlines are part of my job — and in those moments I'm usually the one who keeps the team calm and focused on the solution.

## TPM — 60s
> Pressure usually comes from one of two places in my job: a production incident, or a fixed deadline. Either way I do the same four things. Find the real source — what exactly is at risk? Agree a feasible plan, not a heroic one. Pull in every support available — architects, other teams, vendors. And then execute to the plan, with clear owners and frequent updates. On our ISO 20022 migration, the SWIFT deadline didn't move, and we still went live ahead of it with no service interruption. In P1 incidents, my teams own production support, so I'm the one coordinating — and staying calm is part of the job.

## SM — 60s
> I try to absorb pressure for the team rather than pass it on. When something is on fire, I make the situation simple: what's at risk, who owns what, when the next update is. Then I protect the people doing the work from noise. Fixed regulatory deadlines and P1 incidents are normal in my world, and the discipline that works is the same: a feasible plan, the right support, and steady execution.
