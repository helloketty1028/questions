## 答题逻辑（中文，3–5 步）
1. 先诚实：没有直接的证券 post-trade 生产经验。
2. 再桥接：交易生命周期和状态、报文标准、cut-off、对账、异常处理。
3. 展示基本理解：确认与匹配 → 清算（CCP、净额）→ 结算（DvP）→ 托管与资产服务；对账和报告贯穿全程。
4. 说清哪些迁移不过去：CCP 与 novation、DvP 与结算周期、托管、公司行为、保证金与抵押品。
5. T+1：美国 2024 年 5 月已切换；欧盟、英国、瑞士 2027-10-11 切换。

## 30s
> I'll be honest — I haven't worked on securities post-trade directly. My background is payments, and the overlap is big: message standards, strict cut-offs, status tracking, reconciliation and exception handling. As I understand it, after execution a trade goes through confirmation and matching, clearing — often via a CCP with netting — settlement, usually delivery-versus-payment, then custody and asset servicing, with reconciliation and reporting across all of it.

## ATL — 60s
> Not directly — my background is payments, but the engineering problems match. I wrote the module that processes payment status results coming back from downstream systems, so every transaction ends in a correct, traceable state; post-trade has the same lifecycle problem across confirmation, clearing and settlement. I delivered a payment subsystem with reconciliation at a start-up, and reconciliation and breaks are central to post-trade. I've also just led an ISO 20022 migration. What doesn't transfer directly: CCPs and novation, DvP and settlement cycles, custody and corporate actions, margin and collateral. With the US on T+1 since 2024, and Europe and the UK moving in October 2027, there's less time to fix breaks — so automation matters even more.

## TPM — 60s
> Not directly — I'm from payments. What transfers is how I run delivery on a mission-critical transaction platform: a regulated migration with a fixed external deadline, many upstream and downstream dependencies, and zero tolerance for lost or duplicated transactions. Our ISO 20022 programme went live ahead of SWIFT's deadline and retired coexistence with no service interruption. In post-trade I'd expect the same pressure from market-structure change — T+1 is the obvious one, with Europe and the UK moving in October 2027. What I'd need to learn is the domain: the trade lifecycle, CCPs, DvP settlement and custody. I'd spend my first months with the operations and business teams.

## SM — 60s
> No direct securities experience. What I bring is experience owning mission-critical financial platforms in a Tier-1 bank — cross-border payments and credit collections, serving millions of accounts — and the governance that goes with them. Post-trade is where a bank's operational risk and cost concentrate, and T+1 is compressing the time to fix breaks, so the technology agenda is automation, straight-through processing and resilience. That's familiar ground for me. The domain detail — CCPs, DvP, custody — I'd learn quickly, leaning on the business experts while I do.
