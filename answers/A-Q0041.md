## 答题逻辑（中文，3–5 步）
1. 从问题和成功标准出发，不从产品出发。
2. 用自己的数据做 PoC，不看 demo。
3. 契合度：集成、数据驻留、安全、监管（外包要求）。
4. 多年 TCO。
5. 退出成本（lock-in）；你有选型和 SOW 权限，还负责选完之后的治理。

## 30s
> I start from the problem and the success criteria, not the product. Then a proof of concept on our own data, not a demo; architecture fit — integration, data residency, security; total cost of ownership; and the exit cost — lock-in. At UOB I hold decision or joint-decision authority on vendor selection and SOW negotiation, and I chair governance for four IT service vendors, so I see the full life cycle after selection.

## ATL — 60s
> Technically, I want evidence, not slides: a PoC on our own code or data, the integration path — APIs, data formats, authentication — the security model and where data lives, how it behaves under load and failure, and how hard it is to swap out later. For an AI tool I'd add where code and prompts go, and whether outputs are auditable. Those are the same questions I had to answer myself to get our AI review service through formal architecture and risk approval.

## TPM — 60s
> For me, evaluation is also about deliverability. Beyond fit and cost, I look at how the vendor will actually deliver: their team, their SLAs, how they handle change and incidents, and how they'll fit our release and change-approval process. I'd want the success criteria written into the SOW, so that later performance reviews have something objective to measure against. At UOB I chair governance for four IT service vendors — budget reviews, year-end performance reviews and accountability meetings — so I evaluate with that end in mind.

## SM — 60s
> I evaluate in five steps. One, define the problem and how we'll measure success — before looking at any product. Two, a proof of concept on our own data and workflows, never a demo. Three, fit: integration with our systems, data residency, security and regulatory requirements — in a bank, outsourcing rules too. Four, total cost of ownership over several years, not the first-year licence. Five, the exit: how hard it is to leave. At UOB I hold decision or joint-decision authority on vendor selection and SOW and licence negotiation, and I chair governance for four IT service vendors — so I evaluate knowing I'll live with the vendor afterwards.
